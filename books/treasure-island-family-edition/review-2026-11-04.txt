PART 1: RACE (recommend change, 4)

1. Ch 7, the squire's letter
   Now:      He leaves his wife to manage the inn; and as she is a woman of color, a pair of old bachelors
   Proposed: He leaves his wife to manage the inn; and a pair of old bachelors
   The joke now turns on marriage, not race.

2. Ch 34, the same woman
   Now:      I dare say he met his old Negress, and perhaps
   Proposed: I dare say he met his old wife, and perhaps

3. Ch 34, the harbour
   Now:      surrounded by shore boats full of Negroes and Mexican Indians and half-bloods selling fruits ... so many good-humoured faces (especially the blacks), the taste
   Proposed: surrounded by shore boats full of the people of the place selling fruits ... so many good-humoured faces, the taste

4. Ch 15, Jim first sees Ben Gunn
   Now:      I could now see that he was a white man like myself and that his features were even pleasing.
   Proposed: I could now see that his features were even pleasing.
   The chapter has already said he is a man, so nothing is lost.

PART 8: ERRORS FOUND BY TWO FULL READ-THROUGHS (not yet applied; for both copies)

This book is much cleaner than Swiss Family. Five plain errors:
44. Ch 14: "I turned hither and there among the trees"  becomes  "this way and that". This one is ours, from modernising "thither".
45. Ch 11: "You'll say so, Israel when you see"  becomes  "Israel, when you see"
46. Ch 25: nodding feebly at the corpse "--O'Brien  becomes  at the corpse--"O'Brien
47. Ch 29: "and us no ship."  becomes  "and us no ship?"
48. Ch 20: "as before to speak"  becomes  "as before, to speak"

Spelling. About This Edition says "The spelling is American throughout", and these 25 or so uses say otherwise:
49. sabre, defence, neighbourhood, harbour, traveller, grey, offence, pretence, good-humoured, favourably, manoeuvre, fibres, smouldering, chequered, plough, leapt.
    Recommend making them American in both copies, so the sentence is true.

Small notes in the front pages:
50. "It is also read against the 1894 Scribner printing" becomes "This copy is also read against"; two straight apostrophes become curly.


PART 9: STEVENSON'S OWN SLIPS (not yet applied)

Checked against Gutenberg's text: all of these are in it, so they are very likely his. With Swiss Family you said to fix them all, everywhere, so I recommend the same. This is the more famous book, so you may want the faithful copy left as he wrote it and these fixed only in the Family Edition.

51. Ch 24: the current bearing them "southward"; earlier in the chapter it "sets northward".  Fix: "northward".
52. Ch 22: "the jolly-boat shoved off"; the jolly-boat was wrecked in Ch 19.  Fix: "the gig shoved off".
53. Ch 26: the dirk "hidden in his pocket"; earlier, "in the bosom of his jacket".  Fix: "hidden in his jacket".
54. Ch 26: "the mizzen shrouds"; the Hispaniola is a schooner with no mizzen.  Fix: "the main shrouds".
55. Ch 3 and 4: "about three o'clock" then "Ten o'clock! Six hours."  Fix: "Seven hours."
56. Ch 27 and 33: "two-peaked hill" once, "two-pointed hill" three times.  Fix: "two-pointed".

Readers' findings I would not act on: the hyphen variants (loghouse and log-house), "Dr." and "Doctor", and the Chapter 28 dogwatch in Silver's speech. A reader flagged the footnote in Chapter 21 as broken; I checked the file and it is a proper note.

About This Edition, new wording:
  This is the SchriftOhr Family Edition of Treasure Island, prepared by RFRMD Word Labs LLC and based on the beloved classic by Robert Louis Stevenson. Our faithful edition stands beside it on the shelf, for anyone who wants the book as Stevenson wrote it.

  The story and its people are his. The wording is not always his, and we say below where it differs.

  Earlier printings of this book carried words and remarks, about race above all, that were neither edifying to a reader nor kind to the people in the story. In this edition they have been reworded. Where the story could stand as the author told it, it does; where a remark could not be mended, a kinder and more fitting one stands in its place. The pirates keep their oaths and their rough talk, because they are pirates; the honest people in the story speak more carefully.

  A few phrases about salvation and prayer now follow historic Christian teaching. Salvation is God's work from first to last. Two details of the violence are softened.

The sentence about the pirates is new and mine. The rest follows what you approved for the others.
