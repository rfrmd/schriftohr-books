TREASURE ISLAND (Stevenson, 1883), CONTENT REVIEW, 2026-11-03
Read in full, all 34 chapters. Nothing has been changed.
NOW is copied character for character from our current Edition text; each NOW occurs once in its chapter.
¶ = paragraph number within the chapter, the chapter heading not counted (letter lines in C07 count as paragraphs).
SOFTEN / CUT / NOTE / LEAVE as in the summary file.

------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------
A. Recommended: 18 changes
   Race and nation 5 (Silver's wife twice, the harbour crowd, "a white man like myself", "a rank Irelander")
   Teaching 4 (the dead "watching us", "rest his soul", dying in duty as the ground of hope, "a kind of fate")
   Oaths on your list 9 ("by gum" x3, "in heaven's name", "in the name of heaven", "the Lord help you", "heaven knows" x2, "by Jupiter")
B. Optional, your call: 21 small changes (confound, deuce, "Dutchman", "burn you", "bless your stars", two violent details, and the like)
C. NOTE, no change: 6
D. LEAVE: the pirate oaths that do not name God, the drinking, and the Scripture used rightly

The five I would do first:
1. C07 ¶21 and C34 ¶26: Silver's wife as "a woman of color" (the bachelors' joke turns on her race) and "his old Negress". Same woman; change both.
2. C34 ¶20: the harbour scene, "Negroes and Mexican Indians and half-bloods ... (especially the blacks)". Name no race.
3. C26 ¶5: Jim tells Hands the dead O'Brien "may be watching us". The dead do not watch the living (WCF 32.1). One clause.
4. C18 ¶21: the captain over Redruth's body, "no fear for a hand that's been shot down in his duty". Hope grounded in dying at one's post; Stevenson himself adds "It mayn't be good divinity".
5. C14 ¶22: honest Tom's "Then rest his soul for a true seaman!", a prayer for the dead (WCF 21.4).

The story's own lessons are kept throughout: drink kills the captain, Mr. Arrow and Flint ("Drink and the devil had done for the rest");
Hands dies by his creed "Him as strikes first ... dead men don't bite" moments after saying it; Jim's two desertions are named as folly and
he weeps over them; Jim keeps his word to Silver when the doctor bids him run; the pirates' fear of "sperrits" is shown up as Ben Gunn's voice.

About This Edition: the full note fits (there are racial changes), plus the theology sentence:
"A few phrases about salvation and prayer now follow historic Christian teaching. Salvation is God's work from first to last."

C. NOTE (no change proposed)
------------------------------------------------------------
C29 ¶10 onward  The black spot cut from a Bible ("You’ve gone and cut this out of a Bible. What fool’s cut a Bible?"); the pirates take the Bible as a luck charm,
  Dick prays in panic (C32), Silver jokes him. The book does not side with the superstition: the doctor answers "that comed of sp'iling Bibles" with
  "of being arrant asses" (C30), Jim calls them "the superstitious" (C32), and the verse on the scrap, "Without are dogs and murderers" (Rev. 22:15),
  stands as their judgment. Dick "had been well brought up ... and fell among bad companions": a lesson worth keeping.
C15 ¶22  Ben Gunn's piety: "I’m bound I’ll be good, and I see the way to." Reform by resolve, played for comedy. The book answers it: his pious mother
  was right, and he spends his thousand pounds in nineteen days (C34). Keep.
C26 ¶39  Jim: "and I’ll blow your brains out! Dead men don’t bite, you know," I added with a chuckle. The boy gloats, but the next paragraphs punish it:
  "as conceited as a cock upon a wall", and Hands's dirk pins him to the mast. Keep the fodder.
C26 ¶22  Hands: "I never seen good come o’ goodness yet." His creed, "them’s my views—amen, so be it", is answered minutes later by his death. Keep.
C33 ¶15  Silver finishes the dying Merry: "George," said he, "I reckon I settled you." Shows what Silver is, at the moment he is trying to look loyal. Keep.
C34 ¶26  "his chances of comfort in another world are very small." Plain about the end of the unrepentant; not against the Standards. Keep.

------------------------------------------------------------
D. LEAVE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Pirate oaths that do not name God: "by thunder", "by the powers", "shiver my timbers", "split my sides", "dash my buttons", "Davy Jones",
  "Hang it", "blessed" as a sailor's by-word, "I'll raise Cain". Oaths reported but not voiced ("a villainous, low oath", "with an oath that I leave out");
  C32 ¶12 "It’s Flint, by ——!" is already blanked.
- "God save the king!", "King George (God bless him!)", "God defend the right", the marooned men's "for God's sake, to be merciful": sincere, not vain.
- "by George", "French leave", "gay" (merry), "queer to handle", "arrant asses", "I own myself an ass": period speech.
- Drinking: the rum is the story, and the book is against it. The doctor's "the name of rum for you is death", Arrow drowned drunk, Flint
  "died of rum at Savannah", Jim refusing Silver's cognac (C28). The cabin party's wine and brandy grog are period, unglamorous.
- Violence that carries the plot: Tom's murder, Pew under the horses, the stockade fight, Hands's fall. The squire's "like stamping on a cockroach"
  (C06 ¶13) is his bluster; Dance himself is uneasy about the death. The captain's "as well without that extra mouth" (C18 ¶27) shows his hard sense.
  The marooning, "a cruel sort of kindness" (C34 ¶16), is weighed honestly.
- Scripture used rightly: "go to your own place, like the man in the Bible" (C02 ¶45, Acts 1:25); "gone where the wicked cease from troubling"
  (C07 ¶26, Job 3:17); "hang about your neck like millstones" (C33 ¶37); "you can kill the body ... but not the spirit" (C26 ¶5).
  "you can't touch pitch and not be mucked" (C10 ¶24) is a proverb (from Ecclesiasticus), not given as Scripture.
- The parrot "swearing blue fire" (C10): told, not quoted.
- Jokes about wives ("his own married wife", C10 ¶25; the bachelors in C07 after the change): mild, period.

STRENGTHS: Providence named ("all the gifts of Providence", "God defend the right", Jim "recommended my spirit to its Maker"); Jim's call to Hands,
"go to my prayers like a Christian man ... For God's mercy, Mr. Hands"; keeping one's word at cost; confessing folly; the doctor caring for his enemies
as patients and offering to go to the fevered mutineers; drink and greed shown as ruin; "Drink and the devil had done for the rest".
