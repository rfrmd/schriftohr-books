THE SWISS FAMILY ROBINSON (Wyss), CHAPTERS C01 TO C09
Content review, 2026-11-03. Read in full. Nothing has been changed.
Line numbers refer to swiss-a.txt. Every CURRENT text is copied from the file and is unique in its chapter.

SOFTEN  change a few words so the story stays and the problem goes
CUT     remove a sentence that can go with nothing lost

SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------
30 SOFTEN (3 of them optional), 0 CUT, 3 NOTE, and a LEAVE list at the end.
  Teaching against the Standards   8 SOFTEN
  "Savages", "negroes", "Hottentots", "American Indians"   16 SOFTEN (one of them shared with the onager item)
  Minced oaths                     3 SOFTEN (1 optional)
  Cruelty to animals               3 SOFTEN (2 optional)
  Other                            3 NOTE

The strongest items:
1. C04 The father's Sunday parable. Pardon comes with no Saviour, and the promise goes to all who "molded their future lives to the will of the Great King". As it stands it is salvation by amendment of life. Proposal: the King's Son bears the rebels' punishment, and the reshaped life comes as thankfulness. (WCF 8.5, 11.1, 16.2; WSC 33)
2. C04 and C06 The Lord's Day. The father openly allows "any innocent recreation they choose" (C04), and on a later Sunday evening he calls for running, wrestling and climbing (C06). Like the Sunday sledding cut from Shenanigans, his blessing goes. In C04 three small edits move the afternoon's crafts to Monday. (WCF 21.8; WSC 60)
3. C01 "You know God helps those that help themselves!" The first lesson the father gives at the wreck. Proposal: "Let us trust in God and do our part!" (WCF 5.3)
4. C08 The onager is tamed by biting her ear through, "a plan which, though cruel," and credited to "the American Indians". This is cruelty beyond the story's needs, with a people blamed for it. Proposal: he holds the ear fast and speaks to her steadily (the horseman's hold), and the result stays the same.
5. "Savages" about 14 times, plus "the negroes" (C02) and "the Hottentots" four times (C07, C09). Mostly the boys' fear of an attack. The fear stays; the race word goes ("strangers", "unfriendly men", "raiders"; "the herdsmen of southern Africa").
Also: C02 "Nature provides for all things" becomes "God provides"; C06 the father's white lie to his wife about the explosion, after he told Fritz in C01 "stick to the truth in jest and earnest".

NOTE
------------------------------------------------------------
C01 (line 121)  The mother persuaded into a midshipman's suit for the voyage ashore (Deuteronomy 22:5). It is plainly for safety, and she blushes. NOTE, or LEAVE. Your call.
C08 (line 1953)  Juno's extra puppies "disappeared in that mysterious way". Put discreetly, as in period practice. NOTE or LEAVE.
Luck words throughout ("better luck next time" C01, "our good fortune" C03 C05 C09, "lucky shot" C03, "a lucky day" C04, "by a lucky chance" C09). These are idioms, not teaching. Against them stand "providentially led" (C03) and "trust in Providence" (C01). One NOTE at most.

LEAVE (sound, or fine in context)
------------------------------------------------------------
Piety, sound and worth keeping:
- C01 "if the Lord will, he can save us ... if not, let us calmly yield our lives into his hand"; family prayer at the wreck, on landing and every night.
- C01 Fritz: "we must not settle what God is to do for us. We must have patience and wait his time", and the father corrects his harsh tone. This is also the corrective for "the Lord will hear our prayer! He will help us" (line 23).
- C01 "God will never do so. He has not forsaken us, and we will trust him still."
- C01 "never strike an enemy in a revengeful spirit"; C01 "stick to the truth in jest and earnest"; C01 Fritz asks pardon for beating the dogs.
- C02 "we should not return evil for evil", about the sailors who deserted them; C02 morning prayer, "the God to whom we owe all things"; C02 the mother: "If it be the will of God ... let us be content".
- C03 "providentially led"; "thank God we were delivered"; the boys' evening hymn; "food for our wants is thus provided day by day".
- C04 Sabbath morning worship, hymns, Scripture read (the Bible from the magic bag, "the Bread of Life"); "We can worship here as well as at home" (WCF 21.6). Franz's grace "for Jesus Christ’s sake", and Fritz and the father on thankfulness shown by obedience and "Give me grace to do Thy will" (Heidelberg, Part III).
- C04 Jack rebuked for mocking Franz; Fritz overcomes envy; "a youth of manly character will not depend for happiness on mere excitement".
- C06 "true, genuine happiness consists in that, rather than in mere self-gratification"; "brave to do what is good and right, and to hate evil"; "thank God if their fighting powers ... were never put to the test"; the liane rouge, "one of God’s good gifts to man".
- C07 "each Sunday and its accompanying rest ... especially thanked God"; Jack checked for boasting.
- C09 the anniversary: "gratitude to the gracious God, who had then granted us deliverance, and ever since had loaded us with benefits"; Psalms read, "the Lord our Deliverer"; the Sundays marked in the almanac.
Animals, within survival and the story's needs:
- C01 Jack hits the lobster and is rebuked. C02 Turk kills the mother monkey and the orphan is reared. The jackal fight, the shark, Ernest's penguin ("almost ashamed to kill it"). C03 the porcupine and the margay. C05 the turtle killed, and Jack clubs the penguins, which the father regrets. C06 the iguana killed "without pain", and "a great dislike to killing any creature and leaving it useless". C07 the grouse shot, and Fritz blamed for "a sad and needless tragedy". The buffalo's nose pierced ("some repugnance ... necessity"). The eagle tears the parrot. C08 the bees smoked out. C09 the fish and seals taken for use, and turtles kept on a tether.
- C07 Fritz stupefies the eagle with pipe smoke, and C08 the father smokes out the bees. These are tools, not habits ("the dirty, ridiculous habit of snuffing" is refused in C05).
Other:
- C02 and C05 wine: the father warns about drink and blames himself for giving the boys strong wine (C05). A lesson; keep it.
- C01 the sailors' "strange and often ludicrous vows"; C03 the "journey to the Promised Land"; "There are bad angels as well as good ones".
- C02 "it is woman’s duty and nature, you know, to see after trifles" (the mother of herself, playful).
- C04 Franz "like an innocent little Cupid" (a picture, not worship); "orthodox ghost fashion".
- C06 "as if by magic", "the fairy tale of the wishing-cap"; C09 "as by a magician’s wand", "avenging dragons ... a foul, malignant fiend". Figures of speech.
- Peoples named plainly, without mockery: Arabs, Tartars, Nomads (C03); Mexicans and Patagonians (C06); "the Indians" of South America and the giant cane (C07); "the natives" (C07); Palm Islanders, the Chinese and ginseng (C09). Plant names: Indian fig, Indian pea, Indian corn, West Indian.
- C05 the boys slip away without leave, and the mother is to reprove them. C06 the surprise salute, forgiven. C09 the dawn cannon, apologised for at once.
- C09 the Thanksgiving Day sports. Not stated to be a Sunday; the morning goes to Psalms and praise.
